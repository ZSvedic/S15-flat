# Hrnetička v15 lighting: independent critique (2026-11-19)

Written by a critic subagent that saw only the v15 plan, the light-points draft, the 13 comparison sheets, the lux maps and the lux table. Nothing here is implemented; Zel decides. **[Seen]** = visible in the sheets or numbers. **[Judgement]** = general lighting-design opinion. Distances the critic scaled off the plan are ±5 cm.

## What already works
- [Seen] The ambient preset fixes the hall complaint: hall 275 → 93 lx, corridor 273 → 57 lx (P0 → P1).
- [Seen] Living room in P1: usable evening level (sofa 60 lx) while tasks stay lit (worktop 151 lx, desk 163 lx).
- [Seen] The up-light on the ceiling gives soft indirect light (sheets 16, 18, 21).

## 1. Improvements, most valuable first
1. **Dim the existing hall and corridor ceiling spots before adding anything.** [Seen] P0 is 275 lx in the hall against 53 lx in the living room. [Judgement] A dimmer or lower-lumen, wider-beam lamps bring it to 60–100 lx with no drilling. Keep hall and corridor on separate circuits.
2. **Put every new group on a dimmer and plan the groups now.** [Seen] P1 at 100 % has 300–500 lx patches on the sofa's east end and at the front door. [Judgement] Evening mood is about 20–40 lx, so most groups will run at 30–50 %. Living groups: art lights (8a/b + 9), kitchen bar, TV pucks, desk track, pendants, ceiling. Hall: art lights + niche as one group, two-way switched at the front door and at the bedrooms. Ask for a neutral and a deep box at every switch. Test the dimmer with the real lamp: two LEDARE are only about 8 W, and small LED loads often flicker.
3. **Add low, shaded light sources; the scheme has none.** [Seen] Every new point sits at 148–222 cm with the same 36° lamp, so every sheet shows the same hard scallop pairs. [Judgement] Evening comfort comes mainly from light below eye level. Add switched (ideally dimmed) sockets for table or floor lamps: sofa south-east corner, side table by the east window, bedroom, hall shoe bench. This also covers reading on the sofa (60 lx there now).
4. **Kitchen bar: front edge, stronger, 2700 K.** [Seen] The bar is at the back edge; P1 gives 151 lx at the worktop front, the ceiling alone 60 lx. [Judgement] Aim for 300–500 lx: about 1000–1400 lm/m, CRI 90+, dimmable, opal diffuser, 5–8 cm behind the cabinet front with a small lip. A bar at the back is in direct view of seated diners (eyes at 120, cabinet underside at 150). Use 2700 K beside the GU10 lamps. Check the sink is covered.
5. **Laundry light 10: likely collision; delete it.** [Seen] It sits in a strip of about 25 cm between the utility cabinet and the terrace door, on the hinge side. [Judgement] The door leaf will hit it, and the room already has 425 lx from the ceiling.
6. **Bedside lights 2 and 3 won't work as reading lights as drawn.** [Seen] About 24 cm outside the bed edges at 158 cm; the down light lands on the nightstands. [Judgement] Accept them as mood lights, or use adjustable-head reading lights at 110–120 cm, 15–20 cm outside the bed edge, each switched from its own side.
7. **Room 5 desk lights 13/14: move to ±45 cm from the desk centre, as in Sara's room.** [Seen] Light 14 is 21 cm from the corner; its scallop is clipped and it sits where a curtain stacks. [Judgement] 38 and 128 cm from the south wall still clear a 60 cm monitor. Keep a desk lamp.
8. **Soften the up-lamps.** [Seen] Blown-out ceiling patches above fixtures at 220–222 cm (58 cm below the ceiling). [Judgement] Use a frosted wide-beam GU10 (100–120°) in the up position and keep 36° pointing down. No wiring change.
9. **Living desk (point 5): one lamp on the desk is weak.** [Seen] 163 lx in P1. [Judgement] Aim both heads at the desk or use one lamp of about 600 lm, and keep a desk lamp.
10. **Bedroom point 4: drop the wall point.** [Judgement] If the wardrobe leaves 20 cm to the ceiling, put a socket above it for an indirect LED strip; otherwise leave it out.
11. **Night orientation light in the corridor.** [Judgement] One or two 1–2 W recessed floor washers at 30 cm, on a motion sensor, on the route from the bedrooms to the bathroom.

## 2. The two either/or choices
- **South picture: ceiling spots (option 2).** [Seen] Wall option: three hot scallops on the top edge and a dark lower half; ceiling option: an even wash. The price is less indirect light (dining table 36 lx instead of 62, living room 48 instead of 60). [Judgement] Fine for evenings; ceiling heads can be re-aimed if the picture changes. Use a track of about 2 m, end-fed from the as-built point (39 cm from the wall, steeper than ideal but acceptable). Keep 8a/8b as wall lights so the room still has an up-light.
- **Niche: neither as drawn; if forced, the shelf downlights.** [Seen] The back strips show as a bright dotted line at eye level and flatten the objects; the downlights model them better but give uneven pools. [Judgement] Best: a thin LED profile with opal diffuser under the front edge of each shelf, aimed down and back, 2700 K, 300–400 lm/m, 24 V, dimmable. The driver needs an accessible place.

## 3. Risks for the electrician and daily use
- **Curtains:** [Judgement] lights 1 (bedroom), 14 (room 5) and 16 (Sara, about 28 cm from the terrace door) sit where curtains stack. Decide on curtains or blinds first.
- **Sara's reading light 15:** [Seen] at 128 cm, about 24 cm from the head end and 38 cm from the door opening. [Judgement] She will hit it sitting against the wall; raise to about 140 or use a flexible arm. Check the door swing.
- **Glare:** [Judgement] choose a cylinder with the lamp recessed at least 3 cm. 8a/8b are directly above the sofa's east seats.
- **Art-light heights:** don't drill 8a/8b or 18/19 until the real pictures are chosen.
- **Hall south wall:** settle the fuse-box position before drilling 18/19.
- **Outlet position:** the cable exit is not always at the fixture centre; fix the fixture model first.
- **Colour:** keep everything at 2700 K and CRI 90+; check the existing ceiling lamps; consider dim-to-warm GU10 in the living room.
- **Living-room general light:** [Seen] only 53 lx in P0 with all ceiling lights on; dim for cleaning or games, but the lamp data are assumed.

## 4. What not to trust in the simulation
- Glare is not shown (fixtures don't glow).
- Fixed exposure: the eye adapts, so use the lux numbers, not picture brightness.
- Medians hide extremes; the maps cover floors and furniture tops, not walls and faces.
- Everything is at 100 %; no dimmed scene is shown in the sheets.
- All non-GU10 lamp data are assumptions; wall and ceiling reflectance and curtains matter a lot.
- Bedside lights are rendered as up/down cylinders; lights 12 and 15 appear in no view.
- Sheet 13 panels 2–4: the living room beyond the doorway is black, so the hall looks gloomier than it will be.
- Placeholder pictures are blank white and reflect more than real art.
